Back up what you cannot afford to lose

Protect personal documents and small-team files with separate copies and a recovery plan you have actually tried.

A second copy is a start

Photos, records, and work documents can disappear through mistakes, device failure, theft, or ransomware. Identify what matters and how often it changes. A backup should let you recover an earlier usable copy, not simply reproduce every change to the original.

  • Prioritize important documents, account recovery information, and files needed for your work.
  • Choose a backup schedule that matches how much recent work you can afford to lose.
  • Check what your cloud service retains; file synchronization alone may also sync deletion or damage.

Keep a copy out of reach

Backups connected to the same device or account can be affected by the same incident. Use a protected separate destination and, where practical, an offline or suitably configured immutable copy. Encryption and access controls also matter when backups contain sensitive information.

  • Disconnect an external backup drive when you are not using it, if that is part of your backup method.
  • Protect backup accounts with unique passwords and MFA where supported.
  • Keep encryption recovery keys accessible to authorized people, separately from the files they protect.

Practice restoring a file

A successful backup notification does not prove that you can recover. Periodically restore a sample into a safe location and check that it opens. For teams, document who restores data and which files or systems come first.

  • Keep several recovery points where practical, so damage discovered late does not erase every usable copy.
  • If ransomware is suspected, disconnect affected devices from networks and seek qualified help before reconnecting or restoring.
  • Backups support recovery, but they do not undo stolen data or guarantee a complete recovery.
